ComfortWorks custom wheelchair cushions

A service that scans a wheelchair user with their phone and 3D prints a cushion to fit them.

Project
University project
Year
2024
My role
Solo. Research, service design and app prototype
Tools
Design research, service design, app prototyping

Custom 3D-printed cushions, ordered from a phone instead of a clinic.

The brief

A health design course asked how 3D-printed wheelchair cushions could be easier to get and cheaper. A custom cushion helps with posture and pressure sores, but most users get a generic foam cushion. The custom options usually need a clinic visit, a mailed mould or a lot of manual work.

What I did

  • Researched current cushions, printed lattice materials and the companies already in this field. A printed TPU lattice can match the feel of medical foams, and its density can change across the cushion.
  • Found two gaps. Most services need an in-person fitting or a mailed mould, and a full cushion can take up to a week to print.
  • Designed a service around a phone app. The user fills in a profile, then scans their body and their chair with the phone's LiDAR.
  • Planned how the scan and the profile become a lattice model with softer and firmer zones. A virtual stress test checks how the weight spreads.
  • Split the cushion into sections that print at the same time on smaller printers. A failed print then costs one section instead of the whole cushion.
  • Prototyped nine app screens, and mapped the stakeholders and the full system from order to delivery.

The result

A design brief and service concept, with an app prototype, a system map and a stakeholder map. The app also covers installation guides, cleaning and support after delivery.
