Gas strut placement for a heavy lid

Mount points for two gas struts on a 35 kg hinged lid, worked out from first principles and proven on the install.

The lid never holds itself open, so the struts have to.

Produced at my day job with an industrial fuel-equipment manufacturer. Shown with permission.

The brief

A heavy hinged lid on an industrial cabinet needed gas struts to help it open and hold it there. The struts were already bought, so their force and length were fixed, and so was the hinge. The only free choices were the two mount points.

What I did

  • Took moments about the hinge to find where the lid is heaviest to hold and where it goes over centre.
  • Found that the lid's centre of mass only crosses the hinge at about 107°, but the lid only opens to about 79°. So the lid never holds itself open, and the struts have to do it.
  • Swept the moving mount through the full swing to check the strut length stays between 280 mm closed and 435 mm extended. At full extension the strut becomes the end stop.
  • Balanced the lever arms so the opening effort and the hold-open force both feel right within that stroke.
  • Wrote a Python calculator that takes the lid mass, centre of gravity, hinge and strut specs, searches for mount positions and plots the moment balance.

The result

The chosen mounts worked on the real install. The 35 kg lid closes fully, holds open firmly at the end stop, and the struts stay inside their stroke the whole way. The calculator is ready for the next lid.
